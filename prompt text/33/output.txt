**Stock splits, the fast way.** Nothing is changed yet, as you asked. Only four things on the site are per share: earnings per share, shares outstanding and book value per share in the table, and the price chart. The Screen's earnings-per-share column and its rule that earnings are positive and rising are built from the same figures. Margins, debt and returns are untouched by a split. In order:

1. **Check before adjusting anything.** Open Apple, which split four for one in August 2020, and read its 2019 earnings per share. About $2.97 means EODHD already restates earnings for later splits and that line needs nothing; about $11.89 means it does not. The shares tell the same story: about 18 billion for 2019 is restated, about 4.5 billion is as reported. Adjust only the series that fail the check, since a series adjusted twice is worse than one not adjusted at all.
2. **The price chart is one line.** EODHD's end-of-day rows carry an adjusted close beside the close, and reading that in the chart removes the split cliff at once. Its one caveat is that EODHD's adjusted close folds dividends in as well, so older prices sit a little under what was traded; a pure split adjustment divides the close by the split factors from the next step instead.
3. **The table and the Screen take one call per company.** The fundamentals reply holds only the last split, its date and factor, which the pull already has on disk, so a first cut costs no calls: the rebuild from saved replies can put a splits list of one on every company. The full history is EODHD's splits endpoint, one call per company against ten for a fundamentals fetch, returned as a date and a ratio: about 9,300 calls for what is on file, well inside a day's allowance. Keep the list on the company as it came and apply it when the page draws rather than rewriting the stored figures: for each period end, multiply together the ratios of every split dated after it, divide earnings per share and book value per share by that factor, and multiply the shares by it. Done that way the kept copies, the samples, the store and the Screen's rows all read alike, "Every field EODHD reports" stays raw, and nothing is pulled again beyond the one new call. A live fetch needs the relay to allow the splits path, and the Screen's earnings rule stops being fooled by a split in the years shown.
