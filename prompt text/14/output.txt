v1.13 is built, verified and released: the next report date sits under the company's name, and every line of the table has a graph.

**The next report.** Right under the headline, the company's page now says when its next report is due, from the report dates EODHD lists: the day, whether it comes before the market opens or after it closes, the quarter it covers, and what analysts expect a share. A date that has already passed says so and asks for a fresh fetch, and a company fetched before this version says the same until you fetch it again, since the date only arrives with a fetch. The puller keeps the same date for every company it pulls, and its screen rows carry it.

**The graph.** Each line of the Numbers table carries a small chart mark beside the chapter number. Click it and a popup draws that line in the paper's own style, oldest at the left and newest at the right: a line or bars, by fiscal year or by quarter, where the four ratios built on a year's earnings use the trailing four quarters as the table does. From and To pick any range; Last 5 years and Last 10 years set it at a stroke, by quarters when the chart is by quarter, and setting a range by hand goes back to the shown period. Hover or run a finger along the chart for the value at each point, or press the arrow keys once the chart has focus; the latest value is labelled; negatives sit in claret under a zero line; a company without quarters stored gets no Quarterly choice until you fetch again. The chart is drawn by the page itself, so nothing is loaded from elsewhere.

**The figures.** Under every chart, for the period shown: mean, median, standard deviation, variance, coefficient of variation, low and high with their periods, the trend per year or quarter and how well the figures fit it (R², one being a perfect straight line), the change from first to last, the compound growth a year, how many periods fell below the one before, and the largest fall. Two trend options draw on the chart: a straight line fitted to the period, or a mean band, the mean with a shaded band one standard deviation either side, which is the quickest picture of how steady a line has been. Standard deviation and variance are the sample kind, divided by one less than the count.
