Warren Buffett's Analytics is on every company page that has years on file: a button above the table, between the Add to line and the table, opens a dashboard of tiles.

There are nine tiles, one for each line the finished chapters give a rule for, in chapter order: gross margin, SG&A, research and depreciation as shares of gross profit, interest as a share of operating income, the effective tax rate, net earnings, net margin and earnings per share. Each tile carries the line's name with a link to its chapter, the chapter's TL;DR in the same box as on the chapter page, the last ten fiscal years drawn with the least-squares trend line, and a verdict in the chapter's own terms with a mark beside it: a tick on green when the company meets the requirement, a cross on claret when it does not, a tilde when the picture is mixed, and an i where the book gives no number and the figures are for your judgement, which is the case for research and the tax rate. The verdicts are worked from the table's own figures: earnings per share pass when they rise steadily, the trend up and a straight line fitting most of the movement with at most two down years and no loss, and the sentence says so with the first and last figures; gross margin passes at 40% or more in every year; SG&A passes when it stays within ten points year to year, and is called low and steady when it is also 30% or less throughout; depreciation at 10% or less; interest under 15% in every year, with none at all the best; net margin over 20% this year and in most years; net earnings rising steadily. Revenue, the costs and the other lines Warren does not judge on their own are left out, as you asked. A chapter's link in a tile closes the dashboard and opens the chapter.

Only the chapters written up so far have tiles. The standing policy now says that a chapter written up later which gives a line a rule adds its tile and its judgement as part of writing the chapter up, so the dashboard grows as your notes do.
